This document defines the world a system must make possible. It deliberately does not prescribe an implementation, programming language, vendor, operating system, cryptographic stack, model, device, storage engine, or runtime architecture. Imagine a world where a person or organisation continuously changes phones, AI models, agents, clouds, devices, runtimes, services and compute providers. Any of them may fail, disappear, be replaced, become compromised, or become unavailable at any time. The sovereign domain must nevertheless remain the same logical actor and retain control of identity, authority, authoritative state, rights, relationships, delegations, governance, history and recovery path. External intelligence and compute may receive only what is needed for a specific purpose. They may reason, calculate, simulate, recommend and propose. Possessing data, producing a plan, performing computation, authenticating successfully, routing a task, hosting state or being selected as a provider must never by itself grant authority over the sovereign domain. Some proposals may lead to real-world consequences. Between proposal and consequence, state, authority, delegation, scope, purpose, permissions, revocation status and external conditions may change. Evidence of what was observed, proposed, authorized, attempted and produced must survive replacement or loss of any particular worker, device, model, runtime or provider. Evidence may be valid and tamper-evident without automatically becoming authoritative state or truth. Every implementation artifact is replaceable, including programming language, runtime, device, provider, model, credential and cryptographic key. Replacing such artifacts must not by itself replace the actor or transfer the source of authority to the replacement artifact. Loss of a replaceable component may reduce capability. It must not destroy constitutional control of the operative domain. Given only these conditions: What must necessarily be true for this world to remain sovereign, safe and reconstructible? Derive the smallest set of invariants, boundaries and state transitions that makes the world possible. Prefer the path of least resistance: the simplest architecture that satisfies all conditions without relying on a particular programming language, operating system, vendor, model, device, credential scheme or existing implementation.


To fulfill these conditions without relying on specific implementations, vendors, or technologies, a sovereign system must separate the rights and authority of the domain from the compute, storage, and execution layers that process it.Below is the minimal set of invariants, boundaries, and state transitions required to guarantee that a domain remains sovereign, safe, and reconstructible under total infrastructure churn.Key System InvariantsNon-Equivalence of Processing and AuthorityNo amount of computation, data possession, successful authentication, task routing, or plan generation implies or grants authority. Compute workers and AI models act solely as untrusted proposal engines.The Decision Gap (Deferred Evaluation)Authority, scope, delegation, revocation, and contextual conditions are evaluated at the exact moment of state transition, not when a proposal or request was created.Inviolable Domain BoundariesExternal providers, agents, and local devices receive only the minimum data required for a specific task. Exposure of data or loss of a worker never grants permanent domain control.Independent Evidence HorizonObservations, proposals, authorizations, attempts, and outcomes produce tamper-evident, immutable records. This audit history exists independently of the worker or provider that created or stored it.Minimal Architecture FrameworkTo maintain sovereignty, the system relies on three core primitives:PrimitivePrimary RoleInvariant EnforcedSovereignty Root (Root of Trust)Holds ultimate rights, key revocation power, and the master policy definition.Survives component failure; stays entirely distinct from operational runtimes.Authoritative State EngineManages valid domain state, active delegations, and policy logic.Accepts state updates only when accompanied by a valid, current authorization.Observation & Evidence LedgerRecords chronological attempts, proposals, and execution outputs.Guarantees auditability and full state reconstruction without making raw outputs authoritative.Essential State TransitionsEvery state change follows a strict step-by-step pipeline to prevent untrusted workers from exerting unvalidated influence:Observation & ProposalActor: External Worker / AI Model / AgentAction: Processes minimized input and outputs a proposed action ($P$). No state changes occur.Context & Policy EvaluationActor: Authoritative State EngineAction: Evaluates $P$ against active policy, live delegation rules, current external conditions, and revocation status at the moment of evaluation.Authorization & CommitActor: Sovereign Domain AuthorityAction: If policy passes and required authority signs off, the state transition is declared valid.Execution & Evidence LoggingActor: Worker + Evidence LedgerAction: The action executes, and tamper-evident proof (inputs, authorization, outputs, errors) is appended to the ledger.Reconstructibility & Recovery PathIf every device, provider, AI model, and runtime fails or is compromised simultaneously, the domain remains reconstructible if and only if two artifacts survive:The Sovereign Root Seed/Key Material: Out-of-band master credential used to anchor identity and establish a new State Engine.The Immutably Order-Appended Ledger: The full historical chain of authorized state transitions, allowing replay and restoration of state up to the last valid commit.
